# D-0001 — Light through vanilla's one lookup; sources settled to block centres

**Decision**: The dynamic light enters the renderer through two mixins: the static
`LevelRenderer.getLightColor(level, state, pos)` and `EntityRenderer.getBlockLightLevel`.
Nothing patches Sodium. Rebuilds go through `LevelRenderer.setSectionDirty`. A source is
placed at the centre of its block before the field is built, and only a source that
changed block, appeared, vanished or changed luminance dirties its bounds.

**Why**: Read from the pack's Sodium 0.8.13 jar, `LightDataAccess.compute` -- the one
place Sodium's mesher reads light -- calls vanilla's static `getLightColor`, and Sodium's
`LevelRendererMixin` overwrites `setSectionDirty`, so vanilla's API is Sodium's when
Sodium is there. One hook covers vanilla meshing, Sodium meshing, block entities and
particles, from any thread, reading an immutable field. Settling to block centres makes
the light a source casts a function of its block, so a torch walking across a block
changes nothing and the sections are re-meshed once per block moved rather than every
tick; that is what keeps a moving light affordable on a software renderer and a real
one alike. Verified in the booth with and without Sodium + Iris + Complementary.

**Alternatives rejected**: *A Sodium-specific mixin on `LightDataAccess`* -- a compile
dependency on a mod that renames its packages between versions, for nothing the vanilla
hook does not give. *Exact positions with per-tick rebuilds* -- a rebuild of up to 27
sections every tick per moving source. *Patching `ClientLevel.getBrightness`* -- Sodium's
mesher reads a level slice, not the level, so terrain would stay dark, which is the
pack's situation today.
